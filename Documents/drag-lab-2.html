drag-lab.html shipped six things, and one of them is the complaint: idea 3, the spotlight — every card in the target column scrimmed to 55% except the one under the pointer. Its own cost line predicted this: “it dims the quantity meters and the status spines you are choosing between, and the whole column flickers as the pointer travels.” Ten answers, numbered as they were put in the chat.

Click a grip (the six dots at the head of a row) to lift a card · move the pointer · click again to drop · Esc cancels. Click-to-lift rather than press-and-hold so you can read the notes without letting go. Everything else is the app's: the far side arms when the pointer first crosses the gutter, and the source column dims from pickup.

The diagnosis

  1. The scrim is the one channel that removes information. Five devices are in play during a drag; four of them add a mark to one row, and this one takes contrast off thirty rows to say what the outline has already said.
  2. It is applied to .card. One opacity fades the plant name, the class, the date, the head count, the meter, the spine and the status word together. Nothing separates the cells you are comparing from the ones you are ignoring.
  3. It changes on every row the pointer crosses. Thirty rows repainting at 120ms while the operator is still scanning. Scanning and choosing are different moments.
  4. The hot card is already the only marked card. A 2px petrol outline and a #D1E1E8 fill, on a screen where nothing else in the list carries either.
  5. “Filter on drag” may have overtaken it. It answers “too many rows” by removing rows rather than removing contrast. Turn it on and see how much of the complaint survives.
