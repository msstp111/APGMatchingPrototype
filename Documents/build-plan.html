How the build is run

Every phase is a fresh chat with four inputs

Context stays small and the work stays inside its lane. A chat that only knows about its own phase cannot wander into the next one, and cannot quietly re-decide something already settled.

01

The roadmap

Documents/ROADMAP.md — the shared, stable picture. Domain model, resolved decisions, phase list. Changes rarely.

02

The build log

Documents/BUILD-LOG.md — what earlier phases actually did, and the decisions they took mid-build that this one is bound by.

03

Its phase document

Documents/Phases/PHASE-N-*.md — detailed requirements for that phase alone, with acceptance criteria.

04

The prompt

Copied from this page. Names the phase, points at the documents, and states what must not happen.

Every phase chat starts in plan mode. Plan first, approve, then build.

And every phase ends the same three ways

A phase isn't finished when the code works. It's finished when it has been reviewed by something that wasn't holding the keyboard, recorded somewhere the next phase will read, and looked at by you.

CLOSE 01

Review

A subagent on the sonnet model reviews the phase for correctness bugs and code quality. It reports findings; the building chat fixes what's real and records what it deliberately didn't. Findings belonging to a later phase go in the log, not into this phase's code.

CLOSE 02

Record

An entry appended to the build log: what shipped, what got decided mid-build, what deviated from the plan and why, what the review found, and what the next phase will trip over. Written for someone with no memory of the conversation — which is exactly who reads it.

CLOSE 03

Your pass

The part neither the tests nor the reviewer can do: sit in front of it and judge whether it's actually right. Every phase below carries a checklist of things to click through in the app — no commands, no reading code. They tick off and persist.

The domain in one picture

Many-to-many is the whole point

A meatworks commits space. A farmer offers stock. Neither owns the other, and neither owns the relationship — so the match is a first-class record carrying its own quantity, price, transport company and status. Never a foreign key on either side.

Stack

Angular in front, .NET behind

An ASP.NET Core minimal API over EF Core and SQLite, seeded from the CSV exports, with an Angular client that renders what it is given. The database holds raw records only — every matched sum, unmatched figure and derived status is computed on read.

front end Angular · standalone · signals components Angular Material, themed drag Angular CDK DragDrop style SCSS tokens back end ASP.NET Core minimal API data EF Core + SQLite tests xUnit seed Data/*.csv

One implementation of every rule

The single most consequential decision in this build. Apg.Domain is a plain C# library — no EF, no ASP.NET — and it owns every computed value: both matched sums, unmatched, the derived availability status, the confirm gates, the quantity default and cap, the price lookup, and the week-commencing Sunday.

The API returns DTOs with every computed field already on them, and the Angular client never recomputes a domain value in TypeScript. The moment the same rule exists in both C# and TypeScript, the two drift and the numbers quietly disagree — which is precisely the failure this prototype cannot afford. The week-commencing Sunday is stamped server-side for the same reason: it keeps week banding out of the browser's timezone entirely.

Filtering and sorting are the exception, and are client-side over the already-loaded set — the data is small and filtering has to feel instant. Mutations go to the API and come back recomputed. On localhost that round trip costs a few milliseconds; duplicated domain logic would cost correctness.

The two lists are cards, not tables. The frontend brief calls for cards on a week-banded timeline. Material's table is available if a later phase needs one, but the matching screen's two columns are not it.

Visual language

It has to look like LMS

ExistingAppScreenshots/*.png are four screens from APG's live system, LMS v7. This prototype extends that application's visual language rather than introducing a new one — which is most of why the stack gained Angular Material. Matching those screens by hand would take weeks; theming Material gets most of the way in a theme file.

What the screenshots settle: a petrol-blue top bar with a bold yellow # DEV ENVIRONMENT # flag; a fixed ~200px sidebar with a matching blue header block over a white nav list, active item in light grey, version string in a grey footer; a filter panel of underline Material selects with a stacked × Clear / ^ Hide pair; dense zebra-striped content with small uppercase grey column headers; a circular blue + FAB; Roboto throughout; and empty values rendered as -.

Every prototype screen sits inside that shell — top bar and sidebar present, not stubbed. The existing nav items render and go nowhere; the booking module adds its own. Phase 0 builds it, and Phase 2 samples the exact hex values from the pixels rather than eyeballing them.

Two colour systems, resolved

Record status and quantity fill both wanted hue, and green cannot mean "exactly filled" and "confirmed" on the same card. The split is now settled, and no phase may reopen it.

Hue belongs exclusively to quantity fill — orange under, green exact, blue over-filled, pink over-committed, as the spec prescribes. It shows as a fill meter: a bar of matched against original, at full saturation, with the numeral beside it. Colour, bar length and number are three redundant channels carrying one meaning, and unmatched quantity is the number operators hunt for.

Status uses everything except hue, on the card's left edge: hairline grey for Booked, diagonal hatching for Pending, a solid petrol-blue bar for Confirmed, dashed with the whole card desaturated for Cancelled — each with an icon and a text label. Pattern reads as reliably as hue once learnt, and it leaves the saturated palette free for the numbers. It also happens to match the existing app, where status is plain uncoloured text.

This narrows the frontend brief's "cards should have colours based on their status": status is unmistakable, but carried by weight and pattern because hue is already committed. Easy to swap round if you'd rather — the only hard constraint is that the two systems must not both use colour.

Sixteen decisions that override the spec

The requirements document contradicts itself in places and leaves authoring notes in angle brackets. These resolutions win wherever they conflict with it. They are in the roadmap so that no phase chat reopens them.

01

Over-filling is asymmetric. A space may be over-filled (blue). Supply is hard-capped, so over-committed pink stays unreachable.

02

No Notified in pass 1. Drafted → Confirmed. The status stays in the type with no UI path.

03

Drafted matches are deleted, not cancelled. A mis-drag needs no reason code.

04

Cancelled matches live only in the Match list view — which pass 1 does not have, so they vanish from sight.

05

Availability confirms at unmatched == 0 exactly, not <= 0.

06

Processor Space has no Pending status. Deliberate: partly-matched spaces stay Booked and stay in the default filter.

07

Price is keyed on the Processor Space stock class — processor × PS class × week commencing Sunday.

08

Every drag creates a new match. Re-dropping an existing pair yields a second match, never a top-up.

09

Delivery time is one free-text field. The per-processor variants are deferred.

10

Farmer derives from location. One farmer per location; picking the farm picks the farmer.

11

Plants and carriers are invented — plausible NZ ones, in one config module for a one-file swap.

12

Confirm a space when it has at least one Confirmed match and no Drafted ones.

13

Match quantity caps at remaining supply plus its own quantity — not the record's original quantity, as the spec wrongly states.

14

A mouse is always available. No keyboard drag path, no keyboard-only journey. Material's own keyboard behaviour stays; nothing is built on top of it.

15

The visual language extends LMS, via themed Angular Material. Every screen renders inside the real shell — top bar and sidebar present, not stubbed.

16

Hue belongs to quantity fill; status uses weight, pattern, icon and text. The two systems never both use colour.

Frontend direction

Cards on a week-banded timeline

Both sides render as cards inside the LMS content area — status on the left edge, a fill meter for quantity, expandable for detail, always sorted soonest-first, with a sticky Week of… rail down the left. A flip control swaps the columns, because some people picture animals on the left and processors on the right.

The hard part: a Processor Space belongs to one day, but an Availability record becomes available on a date and stays available until it is used up. The working answer is carry-over cards — the record shows in full in its home week, then reappears at the top of every later week, muted and dashed, labelled available since 10 Aug, for as long as it has unmatched quantity. Scroll to any week and you see everything genuinely matchable that week, not just what became available in it.

Expect to iterate on this. Phase 2 presents it as a proposal alongside anything better found while designing.
